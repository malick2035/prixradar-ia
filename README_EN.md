# 📡 PrixRadar.IA — English summary

**Right time, right place: food price signals for Benin, Togo, Niger and Senegal.**

PrixRadar.IA turns official World Food Programme (WFP) retail price data into simple decision cues for traders, households, producers, NGOs and public authorities: **when** food is usually cheapest, **where** it is cheapest, and **which products are volatile**.

**Status:** prototype (v1.2, October 2026). Proposed pilot: Atacora and Donga departments, northern Benin.

## Why it matters
- Over **54.8 million people** may face acute food insecurity (Cadre Harmonisé Phase 3+) in the Sahel, West Africa and Cameroon during the June–August 2026 lean season; about **201,000** in Benin, where violence affects the Atacora and Alibori departments.
- ECOWAS agricultural policy (ECOWAP) aims to curb price volatility; its regional agricultural information system (ECOAGRIS) is being reactivated (2024 roadmap, new portal under development since 2025) — open, local tools can feed it.
- In WFP data for Benin (2020–2025, same markets): prices rose **+15% (white maize) to +57% (groundnut oil)**; seasonal gaps reach **48%** for tomatoes; prices differ more than two-fold between departments.

## What is new
Existing systems publish prices. PrixRadar.IA adds the missing layer **between data and decisions**: tested seasonal profiles, area rankings down to municipalities, coverage of neglected rural municipalities (planned field network), availability data, and delivery through WhatsApp, SMS and community radio — as an open **digital public good**.

## Rigour
Single tested calculation module (13 automated tests), outliers removed and logged (1.3%), constant-panel comparisons, uncertainty shown, seasonal profiles back-tested on 2025, sources and dates displayed everywhere, two internal audits with public follow-up. An independent review is recommended before scale-up.
